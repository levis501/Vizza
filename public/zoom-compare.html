zoom_to_cursor — desktop behaviour vs. the port

Both panes run identical camera maths ported from shared/camera.rs, differing only in the sign of one term. The white crosshair is the zoom point; the red dot is the world point that sat under it when zooming began. A correct zoom-to-cursor keeps the dot inside the crosshair.

Easiest way to see it: click Auto-zoom in ×12 below and just watch — it steps slowly, and it drives a fixed point on the canvas, so it does not matter where your mouse is. On the left the dot crawls out of the crosshair and off the canvas (an arrow then points after it); on the right it stays put. You can also hover either canvas and scroll by hand, in which case the crosshair follows your pointer.

Desktop (current Rust)

pos += ndc · (1/z₁ − 1/z₀)
camera.rs:206 — the dot slides away from your pointer.

Browser port (corrected)

pos += ndc · (1/z₀ − 1/z₁)
The negation — the dot stays under your pointer.

The auto-zoom buttons apply twelve identical wheel steps at a fixed point (75% across, 35% down) so both panes get exactly the same input — the clearest way to see the divergence without a steady hand. Drift is measured in screen pixels between your cursor and the world point that was under it.

Expect a small drift on the corrected pane when zooming out. camera.rs:106 clamps the camera position to ±2 world units, and zooming out pushes the required offset past that bound, so the anchor cannot be held. That clamp is faithfully reproduced here; it is a separate constraint from the sign, and it affects both panes. Zooming in isolates the sign cleanly — the corrected pane holds the dot at 0.0 px, the desktop one throws it roughly two thousand pixels away.
